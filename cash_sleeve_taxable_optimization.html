Cash Reserve Optimizer

Optimal dry powder asset for a taxable investment account. Optimizes which fund earns the best after tax return without risking a drawdown or significant loss.

Assumptions

1 to 60. How long you expect the reserve to sit before you deploy it.

Distributions

Reinvesting compounds each distribution at the fund's own rate. Leaving them idle earns only the sweep rate, which costs more the longer the reserve sits. BOXX pays no distributions, so this setting does not affect it.

Liquidity-Risk Rule

A reserve has to be worth its full value on the day the opportunity appears. Funds whose duration exceeds your expected hold are excluded, because a rate move could force you to sell at a loss exactly when you need the cash.

Ranking is annualized after-tax return only — no risk adjustment. Expected price change is zero, so yield is the whole of expected return; duration enters through the liquidity-risk rule rather than as a penalty. Distributing funds compound monthly when reinvested; left as cash they accrue only the 0.45% sweep, which costs more the longer the hold. BOXX distributes nothing — it compounds pre-tax internally and is taxed once on sale, so reinvestment does not apply and its deferral advantage grows with the hold.

Yields are 30-day SEC yields as of September 2026 and move constantly. State marginal rates come from simplified bracket tables and exclude local income taxes such as New York City's. BOXX assumes long-term capital gains treatment at twelve months or more; sold sooner, the gain is short-term and taxed at your ordinary marginal rate, which removes the fund's entire advantage. The IRS has not confirmed the long-term treatment and it carries recharacterization risk under IRC §1258.
